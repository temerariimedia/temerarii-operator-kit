# FAQ

Every question here has been asked by more than one candidate. If you are wondering it,
someone else already did.

---

## Money

### What happens after the 13 weeks?

The finalist converts to an **ongoing quarterly contract**.

**Roughly $5,000 per quarter to start** — about **$20,000–$21,000 annualised** — renewed
quarterly. It rises as the brands generate revenue, and it is deliberately not dressed up
as more than it is today.

**Be honest with yourself about that number.** If it does not work for your situation, that
is a completely reasonable conclusion and it costs nothing to say so now. What does not work
is discovering it in week nine.

### What does the 13-week programme itself pay?

| | Round 1 · wks 1–4 | Round 2 · wks 5–8 | Final · wks 9–13 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Technical Operator** | **$150/wk** | **$240/wk** | **$456/wk** |

Someone who goes the distance earns **$3,840** across the season. Rates rise because the
field narrows — 3 operators, then 2, then 1 — so the remaining person absorbs more scope.

### Can I be paid weekly instead of biweekly?

No. **Biweekly, on a published calendar**, with every payday for the whole season fixed in
advance. Payments only ever move earlier, never later.

This is not negotiable and it is not personal — the pay periods are tied to the fiscal
calendar the whole business runs on, and eliminations land on pay-period closes by design.
Changing it for one person breaks the structure for everyone.

### Is there a bonus?

Not yet, and nobody will pretend otherwise. Bonuses depend on revenue the brands actually
generate. When there is something real to describe, it will be described.

### Is this employment?

**No. It is a contract**, fixed-price against deliverables, never hourly.

## Time

### How many hours a week is this?

**That is largely up to you, and it is the actual test.**

The pipeline is already built and already running for clients. Your job is to operate it and
automate what is still manual. **An operator who automates well spends far less time than one
who does everything by hand — for identical pay.** That gap is the whole point of the role.

What is fixed: **two hours every weekday, live on camera**, plus elimination Saturdays. The
rest is yours to compress.

### Do I have to be on camera?

Yes. Every weekday, two hours, live, plus the elimination broadcasts. The work is done in
public — that is the format, not a marketing add-on. **If that is not for you, this is the
wrong role**, and knowing that now is worth more than a polished submission.

## Qualification

### When do I get an offer?

**After you qualify. Never before.**

Several people have asked for a contract before submitting anything. There is no offer stage
that precedes qualification — the order is:

```
qualify  →  selected  →  paid work begins
```

### What actually qualifies me?

**Show that you can drive our pipeline and get quality output from it.** Not that you have
read it, not that you understand it in principle — that you ran it and something good came
out.

Concretely: the exercises, **plus rendered video from this repo**. See `SUBMISSION.md`.

**Setup videos and summaries of the README do not qualify.** Several candidates have sent
proof that the repo installs. That is genuinely a good first step and it is not the bar —
installing is not operating.

### Is qualification paid?

No. It runs on a fictional brand against a local mock, and **nothing you make during it is
used by us or ships anywhere.** That is what makes it qualification rather than unpaid work,
and it is why it is measured in hours rather than days.

Everything after selection is paid.

### How competitive is this?

**Very.** Multiple developers are working through this repo right now, and we are taking the
strongest. Three seats exist in Round 1 and one exists at the end.

### Can I use AI to do it?

**Yes — and you are expected to.** Claude Code, Cursor, whatever you use. This is a role
about operating systems with an agent, so using one is the job, not a shortcut around it.

**Your agent can answer almost anything about this repo.** Point it here and ask. If it
cannot find an answer, that is a real gap in our documentation and we want to hear about it.

### What if I get stuck or run out of time?

**Send what you have, with a note about where it stopped and the error.** That is a genuinely
useful submission. Someone who gets three steps in, hits a real problem and reports it
precisely is more useful than someone who silently disappears.

If it takes more than an evening, something is wrong on our side and we want to know.

## The work

### Do I have to build the pipeline?

**No. It is built and it is running for clients today.** You operate it, automate what is
still manual, keep the calendar executing correctly, and fix what breaks.

### How many brands?

Starts at one or two. **Up to six at once by week 13** if you take the full-time role.

That number is why this repo tests brand-agnosticism so hard — code that quietly works for
one brand is survivable at one and catastrophic at six.

### Who do I work with?

A **Creative Lead** (authors the content), an **Executive Manager** (owns the brand's
success and signs off), and a **Producer** (runs the broadcast). You make the machine run.

You also fix what breaks on the apps and websites tied to the brand — the pipeline does not
end at a rendered file.

### What do I decide, and what do I escalate?

**You own whether the calendar runs correctly. The Chairman owns what it says.**

Escalate: new campaign topics, brand-voice judgment calls, which metrics matter, anything not
already defined in a brand's guideline doc, and adding a brand to the registry.

**Flagging something ambiguous is the job working, not you being slow.** The strongest
operators here catch what the gates missed and then add the gate.
